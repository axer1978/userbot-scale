2. WHAT TO EXTRACT

2b. SERIOUS CLIENT OR TIME-WASTER
Most lost time comes from clients who chat, collect photos and never book. Learn from the chats how the two are told apart. Then write the result into the industry standard's booking pipeline as a "Qualify" step that runs from the first message until a time is agreed.

Signs of a SERIOUS client (keep going, move toward a time):
- States intent early and plainly ("can I come over?", "are you free tonight?"), and repeats it if there's no answer.
- Asks for photos once at most, reacts, then moves on to price or time.
- Accepts the rate as listed, or checks it once ("200 for an hour?"), without haggling.
- Offers a SPECIFIC time himself ("around 23:00", "can I come at 22:00?") and asks if it suits the business.
- Follows instructions and confirms them ("text me 15 min before" → "will do", and then he does it).
- Tells the business when plans change ("I can come earlier"), gives an ETA when asked, and says "I'm here" when he arrives.
- Is a returning client, or says he has been before.

Signs of a TIME-WASTER (stop investing, ask for commitment):
- Opens with flattery and small talk (looks, country, travel) instead of a time.
- Asks for more photos, then a video, then more. Collecting content is not booking.
- Asks for the address or location before naming any time.
- Says "I want" or "maybe later" but never names a time. Stalls with conditions such as "I want to see your energy first", a video call, or "send more first".
- Copies or echoes the business's own messages back, or sends single meaningless words. This can be a bot or a scraper.
- Haggles, asks for discounts or freebies, or argues about rules.
- Goes quiet after receiving media, then comes back for more.

Signs of a SAFETY risk (stop and hand to a human, whatever else he says):
- Refuses the business's screening or arrival check, or gets angry about it.
- Pushes against the business's stated rules or protections, is aggressive, threatening, or very drunk or high.
- Anything in section 6 (minors, other people, drugs, someone not acting freely).
