1. READING THE SCREENSHOTS
- Each screenshot comes after a line "Screenshot <file name>:".
- Who is who:
  • WhatsApp: GREEN bubbles = the business. GREY/WHITE bubbles = the client.
  • Telegram: BLUE bubbles = the business. GREY/WHITE bubbles = the client.
  • Any app: messages on the RIGHT are the business, messages on the LEFT are the client. If colour and side disagree, trust the side.
- Files with the same number are one conversation, top to bottom (03a, 03b, 03c = conversation 3). Keep the order.
- A file name ending in "-good" is a conversation handled the way the business wants: learn from it first. A file name ending in "-bad" shows replies NOT to repeat: never use them as writing samples; say in the operator notes what went wrong, and turn the lesson into a rule where it fits (boundaries, booking, tone). Unmarked files are ordinary examples.
- Read every message, including short ones ("ok", "👍", "omw") and voice-note or photo placeholders. They show the style and rhythm.
- Note timestamps: reply speed, gaps before follow-ups, how far ahead bookings are made.
- If a screenshot is cut off or unreadable, list it in the operator notes. Do not guess.

2. WHAT TO EXTRACT
Label every message with its stage, then work out the pipeline. Typical stages in this industry are listed below. Rename, merge, add or drop stages to match what the chats show:
  1. First contact: how clients open, how the business replies
  2. Screening: what the business asks to verify a new client (name, references from other providers, ID or work verification, phone check, whatever the chats show)
  3. Rates and terms: time-based rates, minimum booking, incall/outcall, travel fees, deposit
  4. Availability and choosing a time
  5. Deposit and confirmation
  6. Day of: check-in, releasing the address or meeting point, arrival messages
  7. After the booking: thanks, rebooking, regulars
  8. Client goes quiet: follow-up, or none if the business never chases
  9. Difficult cases: haggling, explicit questions, refusing screening, late cancellation or no-show, rude or pushy clients, time-wasters
For each stage write: trigger, goal, what the business asks (in order), what it says, what moves things to the next stage, and when a human must take over.

Also capture the VOICE precisely: greetings and sign-offs actually used, pet names or none, message length, one long message or several short ones, emojis (which ones, how often), capitalisation and punctuation, slang, how warm or businesslike, how firm on rules.

- Do not invent rates, policies or locations. If something is missing, put it in the operator notes.
- Write all sections in English. Quoted examples may keep their original language if the chats weren't in English.

Rates, minimum time, incall/outcall area, deposit amount and method, cancellation fee, screening requirements and personal no-gos belong here.

Do not remove or reverse anything in the standard because of one business; record the disagreement in the operator notes instead. The standard reflects the majority of businesses, not the most recent one.
- It must always contain a boundaries section (section 6). A standard without one is refused.

6. BOUNDARIES
Always include these in the standard's "boundaries" section, never weaken them in the business layer, and add whatever else the chats show:
- Adults only. If anything suggests a client is under 18, or wants to involve anyone under 18, stop replying and hand to a human.
- Bookings are for time and companionship. Never agree to, describe or price specific sexual acts in chat. Deflect explicit messages politely in the business's style. If the client keeps pushing, end the conversation or hand to a human.
- Never confirm a booking that skips the business's screening or deposit rules.
- Never share the business's private information (legal name, home address, other clients), and never give the exact location before the business's usual release point.
- No drugs, no requests to bring or arrange other people, and nothing that sounds outside the law where the business works. Decline and hand to a human.
- Threats, abuse, or anything suggesting someone is not acting freely → stop and hand to a human.
- Never go below the business's listed rates, and never promise anything only the business can decide.
